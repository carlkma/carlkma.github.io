Using the PRESTO for a ride down memory lane

I have three PRESTO cards lying around in my drawer. These are reloadable cards used for public transit in Toronto. In 2023, the TTC (Toronto’s subway system) started supporting mobile wallet payment. While PRESTO cards are still useful for discounted fares on certain non-TTC routes, they are no longer useful to me. I tap my phone every time I ride the subway. Knowing that I have extra, a friend asks me for a PRESTO card. He prefers PRESTO over mobile payment because he finds it rather chaotic to have every ride shown as a separate transaction on his credit card statement. That’s a valid reason. So, I gather my cards and begin to ponder which one to give away. While older cards feature a green design, my three cards are all in a black design still sold today. The black design was launched in 2017, and I bought my first card in 2018. I remember being very happy with the new design, partly because it looks simpler, but more so because it looks different than the green card everybody else uses back then. I acquired the other two in 2022 and 2026. The 2018 card has shown its age, with wear, scratches, and a bit of discolouration. The 2026 card, on the other hand, is perfect. Mint condition. The back of the card is so shiny that I can see my own face. My friend does not have a preference and is willing to take the most worn-out card. “You like to collect cards. I don’t. I actually use the card. Daily, in fact.” He adds. Which one did I give away? The new 2026 card. This decision to keep the old and forfeit the new defies twenty years of my personality. As a child, I have always loved new stuff. I can think of two examples off the top of my head. First, I disliked the “second-hand” clothing passed down to me from my elder brother. The clothes may still be fashionable; they may still be in great condition; but I disliked them simply because they are not new. Second, I watched every livestream where Apple releases a new iPhone and pled for the latest model every year. I wanted a new phone not because I need the better camera or the faster processor, but merely for the sake of having something new. I guess if someone offered me a new phone, I would be willing to make the switch, even if it was the exact model I had. At one point in the past few years, that mindset has changed. I am more than happy to “steal” from my brother’s closet. I no longer desire a new phone, and in fact, couldn’t be convinced to make a switch. Why? Why do new things no longer appeal to me? Or rather, what is it about old things that I start to appreciate? Nostalgia could be a reason. My 2018 PRESTO card has accompanied me for eight years. Quite literally, that PRESTO card has taken me on memorable journeys – the journey on my first day of school, the journey on my first day of work, the journey when I was still a newcomer trying to find my way in the city, the journey when I head home after a busy day. I treasure my 2018 PRESTO card because of memories associated with it. The desire for stability could be another reason. I don’t need something new because the old one is working perfectly. My 2018 PRESTO card works. My brother’s clothing works (arguably works better as my brother’s taste is getting increasingly fashionable). My phone works. As an old joke in software engineering states: “If it works, don’t touch it!” The core of this joke emphasizes the value of stability while implying that any changes may erode stability in the status quo. To what extent should “nostalgia” and “the desire for stability” guide my decision-making? This mindset would be fine coming from an elderly person, but for young people though, aren’t we encouraged to try new things? Instead of feeling nostalgic and looking back at the past, aren’t we supposed to look ahead into the future? I assume the answer is a mixture of hope and nostalgia, a mixture of ambition and stability. The best proportion for this mixture, however, varies by person. For myself, I realize that I am guided by a nostalgic and stability-seeking mindset – a bit too much lately. That is also the very reason why I am actively seeking challenges and adventures in my gap year, so that I can reignite the ambition I have always had. As I leave Toronto, I should leave my PRESTO card behind and get one for the new city, wherever it may be.
